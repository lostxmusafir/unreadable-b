Amrita Pritam

✦

Amrita Pritam (1919–2005) was an eminent Punjabi writer, novelist, and poet, remembered as one of the most prominent female voices in 20th-century Indian literature. Her body of work, spanning poetry, essays, and novels like Pinjar, captures the trauma of the 1947 Partition and the emotional complexity of women's inner lives.

In the context of Raj Patil's creative project The Psychology of Love, Amrita Pritam's life stands as a primary case study of companionate endurance. Her personal life was famously characterized by her unfulfilled longing for Urdu poet and lyricist Sahir Ludhianvi, alongside her eventual decades-long partnership with artist and writer Imroz.

Biographical accounts recount that during her years of writing and editing in Delhi, Imroz stood as her constant creative companion, caretaker, and partner. Together, they founded the influential Punjabi literary journal Nagmani, providing a platform for contemporary poetry and essays.

Through her autobiography, Rasidi Ticket (Revenue Stamp), Amrita Pritam documented her emotional structures, reflecting on love not as a static contract, but as an evolving fated journey. This perspective serves as a key thematic foundation for Unreadable Cursed.

Sources & Citations

  • Pritam, Amrita. Rasidi Ticket (The Revenue Stamp). New Delhi: Star Publications, 1976. (Autobiographical details on her unfulfilled connection with Sahir and companionate partnership with Imroz).
  • Imroz. Amrita Ke Naam. (A collection of letters and poems reflecting on their shared companionate life and mutual acceptance).
  • Malhotra, Syal. "Amrita Pritam: A Literary Life." Indian Literature, Sahitya Akademi, 2006.
