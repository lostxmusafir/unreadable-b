Chapter 15

The Language of Hearts

✦

The thread, silent and patient, pulsed in Arjun's chest like a second heartbeat. It had accepted its role as background music, a faint melody that accompanied the symphony of his love for Priya. It was no longer a demand but a whisper, no longer a chain but a memory.

Or so Arjun believed.

Because the thread, like all threads, has a way of weaving itself back into the fabric when we least expect it.

The months in Pune were transformative for Priya. She bloomed in the academic environment, her intelligence and dedication earning her the respect of professors and peers alike. She was top of her class, as she had always been, but now that excellence had a purpose�a clear, focused direction that would lead her to the career she had always dreamed of.

Arjun, meanwhile, was finding his voice as a writer. The program at Mumbai University was demanding, pushing him to explore new forms, new perspectives, new ways of telling stories. He wrote about the village, about the city, about the thread of destiny that had guided him since childhood. He wrote about love and doubt, about faith and choice, about the impossible contradictions of the human heart.

His professors praised his work. "You have a gift," one of them told him. "A rare ability to capture emotion in words. Don't waste it."

He did not waste it. He wrote every day, filling notebooks with stories that drew from his own experiences, transforming his pain and confusion into art that resonated with readers.

* * *

Their visits were rare but precious. During semester breaks, Priya would return to Mumbai, and they would spend every waking moment together, making up for the weeks of separation with an intensity that left them both breathless.

They would walk through the city they had explored together as teenagers, but now everything was different. Now they were adults�or almost adults�carrying the weight of futures they had chosen, dreams they were pursuing, loves they were nurturing.

The streets held memories like containers hold water. Every corner, every building, every stretch of pavement was marked with a moment they had shared�a laugh, a kiss, a conversation that had changed everything.

"Remember when we first met?" Priya said one afternoon, as they stood on the staircase where their books had scattered. "You looked at me like you'd seen a ghost."

"I had," Arjun said. "I had seen the face of my future."

She laughed, that laugh that could wake the dead, and the sound filled the stairwell like music.

Their physical relationship had deepened, as it naturally does when two people are in love and increasingly comfortable with each other. They kissed with more confidence now, more passion, more urgency. They held each other with a familiarity that came from trust, from knowledge, from the slow accumulation of intimacy.

But they had not yet crossed the final threshold. Not because they were waiting for some arbitrary marker, but because both of them sensed that this step�once taken�could not be undone. And they wanted to be sure. They wanted to be certain that their love was not just passion but permanence, not just desire but destiny.

"When the time is right," Priya said, reading his thoughts as she always could. "We'll know."

And Arjun, who had spent his childhood waiting for a destined bride, who had carried a thread of fate in his heart since boyhood, found himself waiting for something different: not destiny, but readiness. Not a cosmic signal, but a human choice.

It was a revolutionary idea. And it felt, finally, like freedom.

Their letters continued, even during visits, because some thoughts are easier to write than to speak. Arjun filled pages with his feelings, his fears, his hopes for the future. Priya responded with equal candor, sharing the doubts that plagued her, the dreams that sustained her, the love that anchored her.

One letter, written during her second year of medical school, stood out from the rest:

"Arjun,

I saw a patient today�a young girl, no older than us, who came into the hospital with chest pains. We ran tests, did scans, and discovered a heart defect that she had lived with her entire life without knowing.

She was terrified. She held my hand and asked me if she was going to die. I told her the truth: that we would do everything we could, that the surgery was routine, that she would be fine.

But standing there, holding her hand, I realized something. We are all walking around with things inside us that we don't know about�defects, weaknesses, vulnerabilities that we carry without realizing. And sometimes, it takes a crisis to reveal them.

I think about us sometimes. About the things we carry inside us. About the defects we don't know about. I trust you, Arjun. I trust us. But I also know that there are parts of you that you haven't shown me. Parts that you keep hidden, protected, locked away.

I don't need to see them. I don't need to fix them. I just want you to know that whatever they are, whatever they contain, I will still love you. Because love is not about perfection. It is about acceptance.

Yours always,
Priya"

Arjun read the letter three times, each reading bringing him closer to tears. She saw him. She saw through the walls he had built, through the silence he maintained, through the careful construction of normalcy that masked the chaos inside.

She did not know about the thread. She did not know about the phantom bride. But she knew there was something�something deep and hidden and powerful�and she was offering to love him anyway.

It was the most generous thing anyone had ever offered him. And it made him want to tell her everything�the truth about the thread, about the destiny he had been carrying since childhood, about the phantom bride who still appeared in his dreams.

But he could not. Because telling her would mean admitting that his love was divided, that a part of him belonged to someone else, that the foundation of their relationship was built on a half-truth.

He could not do that to her. He could not do that to them.

So he wrote back:

"My dearest Priya,

Your letter moved me more than you know. You are right�there are parts of me that I keep hidden. Not because I don't trust you, but because I don't trust myself. I am afraid that if you saw everything, you would not love me anymore.

But I am working on it. I am learning to be more open, more honest, more worthy of the love you give me. You make me want to be better, Priya. You make me want to be the man you deserve.

I love you. More than words can say. More than letters can contain. More than my heart can hold.

Yours forever,
Arjun"

It was a beautiful letter. It was a true letter. And it was a lie, because it left out the most important truth of all: that he was carrying a thread that led to someone else, and that thread was not going away.
