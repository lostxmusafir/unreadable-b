Chapter 56

The Vengeful Path

✦

The vengeful path is the road a wounded man takes when he decides the wound was intentional. Arjun took it without looking back. He would not heal; healing required forgiveness, and forgiveness required a worthy God to forgive. He had declared God unworthy. So healing was off the table.

Instead, he would make the world pay for what the world�in God's name�had taken. Not with violence against persons, but with a campaign: to unthread everyone he could reach, to free them from the lie before it broke them as it had broken him.

"I am not a villain," he told an interviewer who called him one. "I am a warning. Every believer is a future me. I am the future, arriving early, to say: do not trust the story."

The path was vengeful not because it harmed others, but because it refused them the comfort he had lost. In denying them the thread, he was avenging himself on the thread itself�and on the God who had woven it.

He walked the path alone, by choice. Companionship required trust, and trust was the thread's first lie. The vengeful path permitted no fellow travelers. Only the war, and the man who would not end it.

On the vengeful path, Arjun built his monument: "The Unthreaded," a body of work that outgrew the novel, became a movement, a posture, a way of moving through the world as if every soft thing were a trap. He was, by any measure, successful. The fraud he named had many adherents.

But success on the vengeful path is a particular poison. It proves you right, which proves you wounded, which proves you lost. Every convert was a mirror showing him his own scar. And the scar, far from fading, was tended�kept open by the very work that honored it.

He thought sometimes of what Dev had said: that owning your story was freedom. But Arjun had not owned his story. He had weaponized it. There is a difference between carrying a wound and swinging it. Arjun swung.

The vengeful path promised power and delivered only posture. He stood tall, feared, influential�and inside, the abyss still hummed, unappeased. Vengeance, it turned out, does not fill the hole. It widens it, deliberately, so the wound can never close and the war can never end.

And Arjun, on the path, chose the widening. To close the wound would be to forgive, and forgiveness was surrender. So he kept it open, and called the bleeding "truth."

The path bent toward those he had loved. He did not seek them out, but the war found them. Priya, he heard, had read "The Unthreaded" and been unsettled�though she was, by all accounts, happy, settled, a mother, a wife. The book's venom had reached even her peace, and for that, Arjun felt a cold satisfaction he would not name as cruelty.

Tara, too, drifted into the orbit of his anger�not as a target but as a casualty. His public indictment of love and destiny implicated the marriage they had shared, the daughter they had made. Leela, grown, would one day read that her father had written a book against the very bond that created her. The vengeful path did not spare the innocent. It merely claimed they were collateral.

Arjun justified it: better she learn early that love is a story, than believe it and break as he broke. But the justification rang thin even to him, and the thinness was its own punishment.

The vengeful path, he saw too late, was not a road away from the wound but a circle that returned him to it at every step. He walked and walked and arrived, each evening, at the same void.

And the void, patient as God, waited for him to stop walking.

On the path's far stretch, Arjun met an old man who looked like Rama and spoke like no one he expected: "You have waged a long war, child. Tell me, has it brought her back? Has it returned the daughter to your arms? Has it filled the void?"

Arjun, wary, said: "It has kept me from being a fool. That is enough."

"A fool loves," the old man said. "A wounded man rages. Both are alone. Only one of them was ever alive."

The words struck, but the fury, now a fortress, absorbed the blow and returned fire: "Alive? I was alive when I believed, and belief killed me. I will take the death of rage over the death of hope. At least rage is honest."

The old man smiled, sad, and faded�whether ghost, memory, or thread's last echo, Arjun could not say. But the encounter marked the path's end. There was nowhere left to walk but into the darkness he had been carrying all along.

He stepped off the vengeful path and into the dark, not as a defeat but as a homecoming to the part of himself that had always been night.
