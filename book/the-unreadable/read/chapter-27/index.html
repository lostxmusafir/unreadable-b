Chapter 27

The Fracture

✦

The argument did not resolve. It lingered, a bruise that would not fade. Priya withdrew further, not out of anger but out of confusion. She needed clarity, and Arjun, defensive and frightened, could not give it.

He should have told her the truth then. Should have said: "Yes, I carried a thread. Yes, I sometimes feel it still. But I chose you, and I choose you every day. The thread is a memory, not a master."

Instead, he said: "You're being paranoid. Raj is manipulating you. Can't you see that?"

The words, though partly true, landed as dismissal. Priya heard: "Your feelings are wrong. Your concerns are crazy." And dismissed, she retreated to a place where Arjun could not reach her.

The thread pulsed, and this time Arjun recognized the sensation�not warning, not anger, but something like sorrow. As if the thread itself mourned the distance growing between him and the woman he loved.

Neither of them slept well that night. Priya lay awake, staring at the ceiling, questioning everything. Arjun lay beside her, his eyes closed, his mind racing, the stone in his stomach a boulder now.

* * *

A fracture, unlike a crack, is visible. It can be seen, measured, pointed to. And once seen, it cannot be unseen.

The fracture between Arjun and Priya widened over the following weeks. Not with drama�drama would have been easier, more resolvable. But with the quiet erosion of trust, the slow leak of certainty.

Priya stopped sharing the small details of her day. Arjun stopped asking. They coexisted in the apartment like two people sharing a space but not a life, their conversations reduced to logistics: dinner, sleep, schedules.

Raj watched from the shadows, satisfied. His poison had done its work. He had not needed to lie outright�he had only needed to point at the truth Arjun had hidden, and let Priya's own mind do the rest.

Arjun, for his part, grew bitter. He resented Raj, resented the situation, and�most dangerously�began to resent Priya for doubting him. "If she really loved me," he thought, "she would trust me. She wouldn't listen to a snake like Raj."

But the resentment was a shield for his own guilt. He knew that if he had been honest from the beginning�if he had told her about the thread when they first fell in love�none of this would be happening. His silence, meant to protect, had become the very thing that endangered them.

And yet, pride is a powerful thing. To admit now that he had lied by omission would be to admit weakness, to hand Priya the upper hand in a conflict he felt he was losing. So he said nothing. He waited. He hoped the storm would pass.

But storms fed by silence do not pass. They intensify.

The wedding, six months away, began to feel like a question rather than a certainty. Priya's mother, still influenced by Sameer's whispers, suggested�gently, but firmly�that perhaps they should delay, to "make sure everything is right."

Arjun's family, offended, interpreted the suggestion as a rejection. "They think our son is not good enough," his father muttered. "After all this planning, now they want to delay?"

The two families, once allied, now bristled. The wedding, meant to unite them, had become the stage for their division.

Arjun and Priya, caught between the families and their own fracture, felt the weight of it all. The love was still there�neither could deny it�but it was buried under layers of doubt, pride, and external pressure.

One evening, Priya came home from a shift to find Arjun on the balcony, the city lights behind him, a drink in his hand. He looked older, tired, distant.

"We need to talk," she said.

"About what?" he asked, without turning.

"About whether this is still going to work."

He turned then, and his eyes were wet. "It will work if you let it. If you stop listening to Raj. If you trust me."

"I want to trust you, Arjun. But you won't let me all the way in. There's a door you keep locked. And I'm tired of standing outside it."

She had named it. The locked door. The hidden chamber. The thread.

Arjun opened his mouth to speak�to finally, finally tell her�but the words would not come. Pride, fear, habit�they held his tongue.

"I don't have a locked door," he said. The lie was transparent even to him.

Priya looked at him for a long moment, then turned and went inside.
