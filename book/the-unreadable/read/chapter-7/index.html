Chapter 7

First Light

✦

She looked at him with eyes that held no hope. "Dreams are a luxury, Arjun. We can't afford luxuries right now."

"They're not luxuries," he insisted. "They're necessities. Without dreams, we're just... existing."

"Maybe existing is enough right now."

The conversation left Arjun shaken. He had never seen Priya like this�defeated, resigned, ready to give up on the future she had worked so hard to build. It was as if the girl he knew had been replaced by a stranger, someone who had forgotten how to hope.

That night, he did something he had never done before. He prayed. Not the formal prayers of his grandfather's tradition, but a raw, desperate appeal to whatever force might be listening.

"Please," he whispered to the ceiling. "Don't let her give up. She deserves better than this. She deserves to become the doctor she wants to be. If you are real�if destiny is real�then help her. Show me what I can do."

The ceiling did not answer. The city did not answer. The thread, for once, was silent.

But in the silence, Arjun found something unexpected: a plan. It was simple, perhaps naive, but it was something. He would help her. He would find a way. He would not let her dream die.

* * *

Arjun began tutoring younger students in the building, charging small fees that he saved diligently. He gave the money to Priya's mother, telling her it was from a scholarship he had helped Priya apply for�a lie that felt necessary in the circumstances.

"You're a good boy," Priya's mother said, her eyes wet with gratitude. "Priya is lucky to have a friend like you."

Priya did not know about the money. Arjun made sure of that. He did not want her gratitude; he wanted her future. He did not want to be the hero of her story; he wanted to be the one who helped her write it.

The tutoring consumed his evenings, leaving him little time for homework or rest. But he did not mind. Each rupee he earned was a brick in the foundation of Priya's dream, and building that foundation gave him a purpose that transcended his own ambitions.

Weeks passed. Priya returned to school. Her father found work at a different factory, this one paying less but stable enough to keep the family afloat. The crisis passed, like a storm moving out to sea, leaving behind it a sky that seemed bluer than before.

Priya's laughter returned. Her eyes brightened. She was herself again�brilliant, passionate, unstoppable.

"Thank you," she told Arjun one afternoon, as they walked home from school. "For believing in me when I couldn't believe in myself."

"I didn't do anything," he said, the lie sitting uncomfortably in his throat.

"You did everything," she said. "You reminded me who I am."

She took his hand�casually, naturally, as if it were the most ordinary thing in the world�and the contact sent a shock through his body that made him gasp.

"Are you okay?" she asked, concerned.

"I'm fine," he said, his voice shaking. "I'm more than fine."

They walked like that, hand in hand, through the crowded streets of Mumbai, and Arjun felt the thread inside him do something strange. It did not break. It did not weaken. But it shifted, like a compass finding a new north. It was no longer pulling him toward the phantom bride; it was pulling him toward Priya.

Was this what destiny felt like? Not a predetermined path, but a convergence of choices? Not a thread connecting two strangers, but a bridge built by two people who chose to cross it together?

He wanted to believe this. He wanted to believe it so badly that he could taste it�sweet, intoxicating, dangerous.

But doubt was a persistent companion, and it whispered to him in the quiet moments: "You are betraying your grandfather's teachings. You are abandoning the thread. You are choosing a path that was not written for you."

Arjun silenced the doubt, pushed it down, locked it in the box where he kept his fears. He would not let it ruin this. He would not let it steal his happiness.

He was choosing Priya. He was choosing love. He was choosing the girl with eyes like rain over the phantom bride of his childhood dreams.

And it felt right. It felt more right than anything he had ever done.

The thread pulsed inside him, but its voice was growing faint, drowned out by the overwhelming reality of Priya's hand in his, her laughter in his ears, her presence in his life.

He was in love. He was young. He was happy.

And for now, that was enough.

* * *

The months that followed were the happiest of Arjun's life. He and Priya were inseparable�studying together, exploring the city together, sharing their hopes and fears in the intimate cocoon of young love.

They had not yet spoken the word "love" to each other, but it hung in the air between them like a melody that both could hear. Every glance carried it, every touch affirmed it, every shared silence confirmed it.

Priya's parents noticed, of course. But they said nothing, perhaps because they saw in Arjun a boy who cared for their daughter, who had helped them in their time of need, who treated Priya with a respect that was rare in boys his age.
