Chapter 26

Poisoned Words

✦

But "thank you for telling me" was not the same as "I believe you." And Arjun, hearing the slight reservation in her voice, felt the stone in his stomach grow heavier.

The conversation should have closed the matter. Instead, it opened a door Raj had been waiting to walk through.

Raj, learning that Priya was asking questions, escalated. He sent her screenshots�old messages from school friends, vague references to Arjun's "obsession," invented quotes that sounded just plausible enough to sting.

"I'm just looking out for you," Raj messaged. "He might not even realize he's still caught up in it. People don't just 'grow out' of beliefs like that. They carry them. They act on them. Someday you'll see."

Priya blocked him. She did not want his poison. But the poison, once in the bloodstream, does not leave easily.

She began to watch Arjun with new eyes�not because she doubted his love, but because she doubted his completeness. Was he fully hers? Or was there a part of him, tied to a thread, that belonged to someone else?

Arjun sensed the shift. The ease between them developed a slight friction, like a record with a scratch. He did not know its cause�he suspected Raj, but Priya had not told him about the messages�and his uncertainty made him retreat, just slightly, into himself.

The retreat confirmed her fear. The fear deepened the retreat. And the spiral, once begun, is hard to stop.

* * *

The poison of doubt works slowly, like a drop of ink in clear water. At first, the water remains clear. Then a faint cloud appears. Then the cloud spreads, until the whole glass is tinted, and what was pure is now indistinguishable from what was poisoned.

Raj's campaign found new vectors. He spoke to Priya's cousin Sameer�the same cousin who had, years ago, received Raj's whispers about Arjun and Priya. Sameer, now older but no wiser, listened and carried the words home.

"Did you know Arjun believes he has a destined wife chosen by the universe?" Sameer asked Priya's mother one evening. "Some guy from his past was telling me. Said Arjun was obsessed with it."

Priya's mother, traditional in her own way, was unsettled. Not because she believed in such things�she did not�but because a man who carried such beliefs into marriage might, she feared, be divided in his devotion.

She raised it with Priya, gently. "This destiny business�is it truly behind him? A mother worries, beta. I just want to be sure your husband's heart is fully yours."

Priya, defensive now, reassured her mother as she had reassured herself. "He's told me it's over. I trust him."

But the word "trust" was becoming harder to say without a flicker of doubt.

Arjun, meanwhile, knew nothing of these conversations. He only felt Priya growing slightly distant�a missed call, a shorter letter, a hug that lingered a fraction less. He attributed it to her residency pressure, to fatigue, to the normal ebb and flow of a busy life.

He did not ask. And in not asking, he missed the chance to stop the spiral before it became a fall.

The wedding plans, once a source of joy, became a source of strain. Priya's mother, influenced by Sameer's words, began to ask more pointed questions. Arjun's family, sensing the cooling, grew defensive.

"Why is your mother suddenly so interested in Arjun's childhood beliefs?" Arjun's mother asked Priya one afternoon, during a planning meeting that had turned tense.

"She's just being protective," Priya said. "It's nothing."

But it was not nothing. It was the first crack in the alliance of the two families�an alliance that, for any wedding, is as important as the love between the couple.

Arjun noticed the tension but misread its source. He thought it was typical wedding stress, the kind every couple endures. He tried to laugh it off, to keep the mood light. But his lightness landed as evasion, and his evasion confirmed the unspoken suspicion that he was hiding something.

The thread, far from being silent, seemed to pulse more frequently now�as if the external pressure was awakening it, drawing it out of its dormancy. Arjun felt it and panicked, pushing it down harder, which only made it louder in the chamber of his chest.

He considered telling Priya everything. The full truth. The thread, the phantom bride, the dreams, the doubt. He considered it every day. And every day, he decided against it, fearing that the truth would be the final stone that broke the glass.

The breaking point came not from Raj directly, but from a misunderstanding he had carefully engineered.

Raj, knowing Arjun's writing often touched on themes of destiny and fate, anonymously sent Priya a link to a recent interview. In it, Arjun had said: "I believe we all carry a thread to something larger than ourselves. Some call it fate. I call it the pull of the unwritten."

The quote, taken in context, was about artistic inspiration. But Raj presented it to Priya stripped of context, with a note: "See? He still believes. He just calls it by another name now."

Priya read the words and felt a coldness she could not explain. "The pull of the unwritten." It sounded like the thread. It sounded like the destiny he had claimed to outgrow.

She confronted him, the interview open on her phone. "What is this, Arjun? 'The pull of the unwritten'? You told me you'd grown out of all that."

Arjun, blindsided, tried to explain. "That's about writing, Priya. About where stories come from. It's not about a destined bride. You know that."

"Do I? Because it sounds exactly like the thing you said you left behind. And Raj keeps saying�"

"Raj? You're still talking to Raj?"

"He messages me sometimes. About you. About your beliefs."

"And you believe him over me?"

"I don't know who to believe, Arjun! You tell me one thing, and then I see this, and then Raj says that, and I don't know what's true!"

It was the first time she raised her voice to him. The first time the cracks showed as fractures.
