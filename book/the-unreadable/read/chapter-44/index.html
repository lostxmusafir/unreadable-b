Chapter 44

Signs and Portents

✦

The signs, when they came, were subtle. A recurring dream of Priya, weeping, though Arjun had not thought of her in months. A coldness in Tara's smile that he attributed to fatigue. A sense, growing, that the thread's pulse was changing�not contentment now, but something anxious, something warning.

He dismissed the signs. He was happy. His life was blessed. The thread had delivered. Why would it warn him now?

But the thread knew what Arjun did not. It knew the cruelty of fate�that the destined bride had been real, but the happiness she brought was not guaranteed to last. That destiny gives and destiny takes, and the taking is often the payment for the giving.

Arjun's success had made him complacent. He had come to trust the thread absolutely, to believe that because it had brought him Tara, it would keep her. He had forgotten the lesson of his grandfather: the thread does not lie, but it does not promise forever.

The signs intensified. Tara grew distant. Her smiles became rarer. She spoke of "feeling incomplete," of "a shadow she could not name." Arjun, convinced the thread had brought them together, assumed the shadow would pass.

It did not pass. It deepened.

The portents, ignored, became events. Tara's distance hardened into a wall Arjun could not breach. She stopped writing with him, stopped attending events, stopped smiling at all.

"What's wrong?" he asked, frightened now.

"I don't know," she said, and her voice was flat. "I love you. I do. But something feels... off. Like the thread that brought us together was a trick. Like I was a placeholder for someone else."

"No one else. You are my destiny, Tara."

"Then why do I feel her?" she whispered. "The other one. The one you lost. Priya. I feel her between us, like a ghost."

Arjun froze. The thread pulsed�not with contentment, but with a terrible, familiar insistence. As if it were saying: You thought you escaped. You thought the choice was forgotten. But the choice leaves a mark.

He did not understand. He had married his destiny. Tara was the destined bride. Priya was the past. Why would Tara feel Priya's ghost?

The thread, for the first time in years, felt not like a guide but like a riddle with no answer.
